Process architecture

3.0 Outcome Readiness Management

For every candidate action, determine whether enough is known, whether a human must own the consequence, whether hard controls permit execution and what evidence gap actually matters.

Process view

Required behaviour

  • Identify candidate actions after every material Claim State change.
  • Assess evidence sufficiency for the action, not for the claim as a whole.
  • Acquire the minimum decision-relevant evidence when a material gap exists.
  • Route to human judgement for consequence, ambiguity, authority, vulnerability or controlled exceptions.
  • Apply deterministic controls after evidence and human requirements are satisfied.
  • Release one, several or no actions.

Relevant controls

These are the controls most directly connected to this process area. Applicability still depends on product, Code subscription, licence, jurisdiction and entity context.

CODE CTRL-04

Claim decision timing

Decision timing is monitored from claim receipt and from completion of relevant enquiries; extended circumstances and alternative timetables are handled explicitly.

Basis: GICOP 76-78, 83-84

LAW CTRL-09

AFS claims handling conduct

Claims handling is a regulated financial service. The operating model must support efficient, honest and fair service, competent representatives, supervision and disclosure obligations.

Basis: Corporations Act s912A; ASIC INFO 253

DESIGN CTRL-14

AI consequence boundary

AI may recommend and summarise. Rights-affecting, adverse, high-consequence or otherwise controlled actions require deterministic gates and human accountability where the control model says so.

Basis: Blueprint control
