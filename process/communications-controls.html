Process architecture

5.0 / 6.0 Continuous lifecycle services

Communication and controls run alongside the claim. They do not wait for the operational process to reach a special “compliance” stage.

Process view

Required behaviour

  • Customer communications respond to state change, timer, enquiry, evidence request, appointment, decision and complaint.
  • Control services monitor timeframes, information relevance, decision basis, suppliers, investigation, privacy, authority and audit.
  • A control event can create a task, stop one action, change routing or require communication.
  • A complaint can exist while claim handling continues. IDR must not become a second disconnected version of the claim.
  • Customer context, including vulnerability and communication needs, is maintained as part of Claim State.

Relevant controls

These are the controls most directly connected to this process area. Applicability still depends on product, Code subscription, licence, jurisdiction and entity context.

CODE CTRL-01

Claim information relevance

Only ask for and rely on claim information relevant to the decision; explain why it is needed; where further information/assessment is required, address the paragraph 68 timing and one-request best-endeavours requirements.

Basis: GICOP 67-68

CODE CTRL-02

Claim progress communication

At least 20-business-day progress updates and response to routine progress enquiries within 10 business days, subject to Code qualifications/alternative timetables.

Basis: GICOP 70-71

CODE CTRL-03

External appointment communication

Notify specified assessor/adjuster/investigator appointments within 5 business days, control expert timeframes/qualification and communicate expert delay where required.

Basis: GICOP 72-75

CODE CTRL-04

Claim decision timing

Decision timing is monitored from claim receipt and from completion of relevant enquiries; extended circumstances and alternative timetables are handled explicitly.

Basis: GICOP 76-78, 83-84

CODE CTRL-05

Adverse/partial decision reasons

Written communication identifies unaccepted aspects, reasons, information/report rights and complaints process where a claim is denied or not paid in full.

Basis: GICOP 81-82

CODE CTRL-07

Vulnerability support

Customer vulnerability is a dynamic process control affecting communication, support, representation, identification and interpreter handling.

Basis: GICOP 91-103

CODE CTRL-08

Service supplier accountability

Supplier use does not remove insurer accountability; appointment, subcontracting, conduct, complaints and authorised-repair quality controls remain with the insurer.

Basis: GICOP 35-41, 86-87

LAW CTRL-09

AFS claims handling conduct

Claims handling is a regulated financial service. The operating model must support efficient, honest and fair service, competent representatives, supervision and disclosure obligations.

Basis: Corporations Act s912A; ASIC INFO 253

REG CTRL-10

Complaint recognition and IDR

Expressions of dissatisfaction must be recognised/recorded and managed through IDR; standard complaints generally require an IDR response within 30 calendar days, subject to exceptions and delay-notification requirements.

Basis: ASIC RG 271; REP 802

LAW/ASIC CTRL-11

Cash settlement disclosure and fairness

Where a Cash Settlement Fact Sheet is legally required, issue it at the offer point with prescribed content. Settlement design must also support realistic, transparent amounts and informed customer choice.

Basis: INFO 253; ASIC 2026 cash-settlement review
