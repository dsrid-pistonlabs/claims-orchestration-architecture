Context

Do not put AI on top of the queue

The target is not a faster version of today. It is a claims operating model that removes sequence where sequence is not real, while preserving the decisions and controls that genuinely matter.

The problem is not that claims are slow

Speed is a symptom. The deeper problem is that the process often assumes sequence where the underlying decision does not. Liability may be unresolved while first-party repair is obvious. Recovery may have work to do after the customer outcome is complete. A supplier delay may require intervention without changing coverage. A customer can need urgent support before every administrative field is resolved.

If those activities genuinely depend on each other, sequence is correct. If they do not, the queue is an operating-model choice.

A working definition of straight-through processing

Architecture position, not a regulatory definition

In this publication, STP means an end-to-end claim outcome completed without human touch. Automating individual steps inside a claim that still requires manual routing or decisioning is step automation, not STP.

This distinction matters because a programme can report high automation and still have almost no end-to-end no-touch outcomes. The target architecture therefore measures action readiness and full-claim outcomes separately from task automation.

Three assumptions worth challenging

“We need the whole claim understood first.”

Usually false. We need enough evidence for the specific action being considered. Different actions have different evidence needs.

“A confidence threshold can automate the decision.”

Incomplete. Confidence is one input. Authority, consequence, evidence quality, legal/policy basis, customer impact and hard controls remain separate questions.

“Putting agents on each stage is transformation.”

No. If the same stages, queues, joins and ownership boundaries remain, the organisation has automated the current process rather than redesigned it.

“Parallel means everything fires at once.”

No. Parallelism is used where independence is real. Sequence remains where customer choice, evidence, authority, safety, law or irreversibility creates a genuine dependency.

The question the orchestrator should keep asking

What can safely, lawfully and usefully happen now?

That question is deliberately narrower than “what stage is the claim in?” It forces the design to reason about evidence, uncertainty, authority and customer impact at the action level.
