Claims Orchestration Reference Architecture

A practical future-state operating model for general insurance claims. Built around persistent Claim State, parallel intelligence, action-specific readiness, continuous customer communication and explicit control.

The claim should stop travelling. Evidence, decisions, people and actions should move around the claim.

This architecture is a practical answer to a simple problem: most claims processes still behave like a relay race. The customer lodges a claim, then the file moves through queues until enough departments have touched it. AI can make each queue faster. That does not make the operating model better.

Scope and accountability

This is a reference architecture, not a declaration that any implementation is compliant. Product, policy, licence, Code, jurisdiction, delegation, privacy, prudential and legal applicability must be validated before production use.

The core design

1.0

Claim Establishment and Orientation

Create the claim, establish customer/policy/event context, identify immediate support needs and create the first auditable Claim State.

Boundary: A claim identity, initial facts and customer context exist. It does not mean the claim is fully understood.

2.0

Claim Intelligence Management

Develop coverage, damage/quantum, liability, integrity, customer context, recovery and economics findings independently as evidence changes.

Boundary: Each domain publishes findings and unknowns. There is no global join.

3.0

Outcome Readiness Management

Evaluate each candidate action on its own evidence sufficiency, uncertainty, consequence, authority, human-accountability and hard-control requirements.

Boundary: Readiness is action-specific, not claim-wide.

4.0

Outcome Execution and Fulfilment

Execute only eligible actions and return success, failure and exception events to Claim State.

Boundary: Actions can run concurrently when their prerequisites are independent.

5.0

Customer Communication and Support

Maintain coherent customer communication, requests, updates, vulnerability/hardship support, decision communication and complaint recognition.

Boundary: Communication is part of the process, not an afterthought.

6.0

Regulatory, Risk and Control Management

Operate obligation timers, privacy, authority, investigation, supplier, complaint, decision-basis and audit controls continuously.

Boundary: Controls can permit, stop, redirect or escalate individual actions.

7.0

Resolution, Transition and Closure

Close the customer-facing claim when the outcome and mandatory obligations are complete, while transitioning separable residual work.

Boundary: Recovery, litigation or reconciliation need not keep the customer claim artificially open.

What is different

TodayTarget state
The claim moves from stage to stage.The claim remains a persistent state. Work assembles around it.
A stage tends to wait for the prior stage to finish.Independent intelligence, communication and controls progress concurrently.
Readiness is inferred from stage completion.Readiness is assessed for each candidate action against evidence, consequence, authority and controls.
Humans appear because a team owns a stage.Humans appear because judgement, authority, vulnerability, consequence or exception requires them.
Regulatory controls are often buried in procedures.Obligations and timers are explicit, event-driven controls in the orchestration model.
Automation means making steps faster.Automation follows process redesign. Unnecessary work is removed before it is automated.
