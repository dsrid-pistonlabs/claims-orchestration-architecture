Reference architecture

Readiness belongs to the action

The decision architecture replaces stage completion with an explicit test of evidence, human accountability, authority and control for each candidate action.

Evidence sufficiency

Evidence sufficiency is deliberately decision-specific. The question is not “is the claim fully known?” It is “is there a material unknown that could reasonably change this action?”

TestQuestion
RelevanceWhich evidence is actually required for this action?
ReliabilityIs the evidence reliable enough for the consequence?
Material unknownWhat remains unknown and could it change the outcome?
ConsequenceWhat happens if the action is wrong?
ReversibilityCan the action be reversed or corrected without material harm?
Customer impactWould delay or error materially disadvantage the customer?

Progressive commitment

The architecture allows the insurer to commit incrementally. Acknowledge. Assist. Appoint. Repair. Make an interim payment. Settle. Each action has its own readiness conditions. That avoids the false choice between “fully automated claim” and “fully manual claim”.

Key boundary

Confidence can support the sufficiency assessment. It cannot substitute for authority, evidence relevance, a policy/legal basis or a required human decision.
