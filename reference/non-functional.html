Reference architecture

The process is only as good as its failure behaviour

A future-state claims model that cannot explain decisions, survive dependency failure or prevent duplicate side effects is not production architecture.

Non-functional requirements

IDRequirementArchitecture intent
NFR-01AuditabilityReconstruct material decisions and actions from evidence, state, rules/models, human rationale, authority and communications.
NFR-02IdempotencyPrevent duplicate side effects across payment, supplier instructions, appointments and outbound communications.
NFR-03State consistencyUse versioning/concurrency controls so independent services cannot silently overwrite newer Claim State.
NFR-04Data minimisationCollect, expose and retain personal information only to the extent permitted and reasonably necessary for the purpose.
NFR-05Access controlApply least privilege and context-sensitive access to claim, vulnerability, investigation and complaint data.
NFR-06Information securityFit the insurer’s security architecture, logging, vulnerability management, testing and incident response obligations.
NFR-07Operational resilienceDefine critical operations, tolerances, fallback modes, dependency monitoring, continuity and supplier failure paths.
NFR-08ExplainabilityMaterial recommendations and decisions must expose evidence and reasoning in a form a competent human can interrogate.
NFR-09Human overrideWhere human accountability is required, provide a real override path with rationale capture and downstream state correction.
NFR-10ObservabilityMonitor process latency, control failures, model drift, retries, supplier delay, complaint signals and customer-impact metrics.
NFR-11VersioningPersist rule, prompt, model, service, policy-wording and workflow versions relevant to material decisions.
NFR-12Graceful degradationWhen an AI/service/integration is unavailable, fall back to a safe degraded process rather than losing claim control.

Prudential fit

For an APRA-regulated general insurer, operational resilience and information security are not optional qualities to bolt on after the process works. The architecture should be designed within the entity’s CPS 230 and CPS 234 frameworks, including critical-operation tolerances, service-provider risk, continuity, information-security controls and incident management.
