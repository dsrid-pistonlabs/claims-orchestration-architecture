Operating model

Principles before technology

These principles are deliberately opinionated. They exist to stop the implementation from drifting back into a linear current-state process with more automation around it.

Twelve design principles

PR-01
The claim stops travelling
The claim is a persistent state. Evidence, decisions, people and actions move around it. Organisational ownership is not the primary process state.
PR-02
Parallel where independence is real
Coverage, damage, liability, integrity, customer context, recovery, economics, communications and controls can progress concurrently when they are not genuinely dependent.
PR-03
Parallel does not mean uncontrolled
Analysis can run concurrently. Customer-affecting action still earns the right to proceed through evidence, authority, consequence and control checks.
PR-04
Confidence is not authority
A confidence score is evidence about uncertainty. It is not delegated authority, a legal conclusion or permission to take an irreversible action.
PR-05
Decisions remain, the mechanism changes
Coverage, quantum, liability, settlement and complaint decisions do not disappear. What changes is how evidence reaches them and how independently outcomes can progress.
PR-06
Ask for less, not more
Request information only for a material decision gap. Prefer approved information already held or reasonably available before burdening the customer again.
PR-07
Release actions individually
A claim can be ready for repair, customer support and recovery while another decision remains unresolved. Readiness belongs to the action.
PR-08
Humans resolve consequence and ambiguity
Human work is targeted to rights-affecting outcomes, ambiguity, vulnerability, high consequence, investigation, authority and exceptions. Legacy ownership is not enough reason to create a task.
PR-09
Communication is part of the process
Customer communication runs continuously and can be triggered by state change, timer, enquiry, evidence request, appointment or decision.
PR-10
Controls run continuously
Regulatory, privacy, authority, complaint and supplier controls are not a final gate. They operate alongside the claim and can change routing at any point.
PR-11
Every material decision is reconstructable
The organisation must be able to show what was known, what evidence was relied on, which rule/model/human acted, why the action was permitted and what the customer was told.
PR-12
Do not automate a bad queue
Remove unnecessary sequence and hand-offs before automating. If every current-state step survives, transformation probably did not happen.

The two guardrails that matter most

Confidence is not authority

Models can support evidence interpretation and recommendations. They do not create delegated authority and should not be allowed to override deterministic legal, policy, financial or prudential controls.

Parallel is not uncontrolled

Run independent analysis in parallel. Release customer-affecting actions only when that action has earned the right to proceed.
